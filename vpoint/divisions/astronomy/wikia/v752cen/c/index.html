V752 Centauri (AB) c is an exoplanet orbiting the W Ursae Majoris type contact stars V752 A & B in the constellation Centaurus. It is a sub-Neptune, meaning it is larger than Earth but is significantly smaller (in mass and radius) than the gas giants of the Solar System.

Physical characteristics

Atmosphere

Studies indicate the planet may have an atmosphere composed mainly of water vapor.

Candidate exomoons

Researchers using the Great Oberon Relay Telescope published new observations of the exoplanet V752 Centauri (AB) c, which revealed three independent lines of evidence indicative of a trio of Mars-sized icy moons. These were a series of distinct Transit Timing Variation signatures that indicated the gravitational pull of multiple satellite masses, and additional photometric dips that corresponded to the passage of three separate Mars-radius bodies.

Potential ring system

The planet has an anomalously large radius (19.05 R🜨) for a planet of its size and temperature. This radius, combined with its measured mass of (4.27 M🜨), suggest that its core has a maximum mass of 3 M🜨 and subsequently the planet has an envelope fraction of 75% or greater. This envelope fraction is larger than would be possible in the core accretion model of planet formation for a planet with its core mass, which is consistent with the hypothesis that the planet's radius may be observed to be larger than it actually is due to an optically thick ring system (which would reduce its true radius to 2.88 R🜨).
