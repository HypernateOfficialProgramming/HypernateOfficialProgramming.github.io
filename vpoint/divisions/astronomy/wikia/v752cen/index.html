V752 Centauri (HD 101799) is multiple star system and variable star in the constellation of Centaurus.
Its variability was discovered by Howard Bond in 1970.

V752 Centauri is a contact binary of the W Ursae Majoris type, composed of two F-type stars with a combined spectral type of F7/G0(V).
Individually, the components have been classified as F8 + F5, and F8 + F7.5. With effective temperatures of 5,955 and 6,221 K, the system is classified as a W Ursae Majoris variable of subtype W, where the secondary star is hotter than the primary; for this reason, the primary eclipses are caused by the occultation of the secondary star.
The system has an orbital period of only 0.3702 days and a separation of 2.59 solar radii.

The system's spectrum shows the spectral lines of a third star, which seems to be a K-type main sequence star. This third star is itself a spectroscopic binary with a period of 5.147 days, with a small companion that is probably an M-type red dwarf. The V752 Centauri system is thus composed of four stars, with two binary pairs that orbit each other.

V752 Centauri (AB) b (informally Centaurus b) is a exoplanet discovered in 42,113,377 (LUT-1). It has an estimated minimum mass of 0.77 M🜨 (Earth masses) and orbits approximately 0.17 AU from AB. Because of this close proximity, it is likely tidally locked, causing dayside temperatures to soar near 1800 K (1526.85°C; 2780.33°F).
Astrophysicists theorize that Centaurus b is a carbon planet; intense thermal radiation blasts diatomic carbon from its surface, producing a distinct green light spectrum.

V752 Centauri (AB) c is an exoplanet discovered in 42,113,378 (LUT-1). It is likely a mini-Neptune about 4 times as massive as Earth, and orbits at roughly 1.97 AU.
The planet may have a ring system with a radius of around 1.7 RJ and three moons, likely icy in composition.

V752 Centauri (AB) d is likely a terrestrial planet with a single moon, predicted by transit timing variations. Data indicates that V752 Centauri (AB) d could be a chlorine planet (A planet with significant amounts of free chlorine or hydrochloric acid), making it the first of its kind to be discovered.

V752 Centauri (CD) b is a exoplanet discovered in 42,113,360 (LUT-1). It is the only known major body around V752 Centauri (CD).
It orbits at roughly 0.47 AU from its stars and has one known moon: V752 Centauri (CD) b I.

The two are considered a binary system because the barycenter of their orbits does not lie within either body, and are tidally locked to each other.

Both bodies are candidates in the search for extraterrestrial life as both of them lie within the habitable zone of their stars (where the temperature allows water to stay liquid).

Oxygen and methane have recently been detected in the atmosphere of V752 Centauri (CD) b I, suggesting the presence of photosyntetic lifeforms.
