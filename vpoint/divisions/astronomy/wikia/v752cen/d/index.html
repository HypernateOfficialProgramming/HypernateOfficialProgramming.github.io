V752 Centauri (AB) d (formally named Khlorus) is an exoplanet orbiting the W Ursae Majoris type contact stars V752 A & B in the constellation Centaurus. Discovered by the Great Oberon Realy Telescope, it is likely the only example of a chlorine planet in the galaxy.

Physical properties

Mass, radius, and composition

V752 Centauri (AB) d's radius was initially thought to be 1.431 times that of Earth, but has since been revised to 1.392 R🜨 as of 2023. In the absence of an atmosphere, its equilibrium temperature (assuming an Earth-like albedo) would be approximately 279 K (6 °C), compared with Earth's 255 K (−18 °C). V752 Centauri (AB) d may be a chlorine planet, a planet with significant amounts of free chlorine or hydrochloric acid.

Surface conditions

The equilibrium temperature of V752 Centauri (AB) d is estimated to be about 186 K (-87°C / -125°F).

Habitability

The habitability of V752 Centauri (AB) d is constrained by its exotic atmospheric composition and frigid environment. As a putative chlorine planet, the surface and atmosphere are expected to be highly corrosive, dominated by free chlorine or hydrochloric acid. For Earth-like carbon-based life, these conditions are lethal due to the destruction of organic cellular structures. However, theoretical models suggest that alternative biochemical pathways could exist.

In place of traditional oxygenic photosynthesis, organisms on V752 Centauri (AB) d would likely utilize chlorinic photosynthesis, mapping a biological cycle where chlorine acts as the primary electron acceptor. Rather than splitting water, specialized autotrophs could split hydrochloric acid to generate metabolic energy, yielding free chlorine gas as a byproduct.

With an equilibrium temperature of approximately 186 K (−87°C / −125°F), the planet sits far outside the conventional liquid-water habitable zone. At these temperatures, water remains permanently frozen as crystalline ice bedrock. If any liquid phase exists on the surface to serve as a biological solvent, it would likely consist of low-freezing point fluids. Concentrated aqueous solutions of hydrochloric acid can remain liquid down to 187 K (−86°C), matching the planet's equilibrium conditions perfectly. Any hypothetical life would therefore need to be hyper-acidophilic, thriving in highly concentrated cryogenic acid pools. A thick atmosphere rich in greenhouse gases could potentially boost V752 Centauri (AB) d's temperature to more temperate levels.
