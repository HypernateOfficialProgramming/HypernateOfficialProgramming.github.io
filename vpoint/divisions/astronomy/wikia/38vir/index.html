38 Virginis is an F-type main sequence star in the constellation of Virgo. It has the proper name Arges, as selected in the NameExoWorlds campaign by the Afari Federation. With an apparent magnitude of 6.135, it is very close to the average threshold for naked eye visibility, and can only be viewed from sufficiently dark skies, far from light pollution. It is around 109.1 light years distant from the Earth.

It is notable for hosting a planet with an inhabited moon.

Nomenclature

The name 38 Virginis derives from the star being the 38th star in order of right ascension catalogued in the constellation Virgo by Flamsteed in his star catalogue. The designation b of 38 Virginis b derives from the order of discovery and is given to the first planet orbiting a given star, followed by the other lowercase letters of the alphabet. Later on, most of the planets and moons of 38 Virginis were named after various mythological figures. The star itself was named Arges, one of the three Hesiodic Cyclopes in Greek mythology.'

Characteristics

The star is metal-rich, with a metallicity ([Fe/H]) of 0.07 dex, or 117% the solar amount. Its luminosity (L☉) is 3.22 times that of the Sun.

A companion star is cataloged in the CCDM at a separation of half an arcsecond.

Planetary system

Main articles: Galena, Thedus, Galitea and Eruantur

38 Virginis is known to host four planets, three of which hosting a thick envelope consisting of volatiles.

In 2016, the star was known to host one exoplanet (38 Virginis b, later named Galitea). It had a mass of around 4.5 times that of the planet Jupiter, an orbital period of 738.916 days (2.02 years) and a relatively low eccentricity out of any long-period giant exoplanet discovered at the time, with an eccentricity of 0.027.

The planet lies within the host star's habitable zone. As a consequence, one of its moons has the conditions to support life.

38 Virginis is notable for sharing similarities with the Solar System, in which rocky planets are nearer the star and gas giants farther away.

Near resonances

The period ratio c:b is close to 7:5 (7.16:5).
