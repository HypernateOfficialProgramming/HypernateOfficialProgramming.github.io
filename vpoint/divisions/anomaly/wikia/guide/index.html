An anomaly is any observation, object, event, or phenomenon that deviates from established expectations or accepted models.

Examples of which include:

  • A planet suddenly brightening and changing orbit
  • A distant spacecraft's signal suddenly vanishing
  • Strange formations on the sky of a far-off world

The goal of this division is to catalog, analyze, and eventually explain these peculiar phenomena.

  • The planet changing orbits could have been a result of a planetary collision.
  • The spacecraft could have run out of power.
  • The formations in the sky could just be an exotic form of aurora caused by a stellar outburst.

Almost every catalogued anomaly is eventually explained without requiring incorporeal or otherwise unknown mechanisms.

When an anomaly breaks a certain area of known knowledge, that area is expanded upon until it can accommodate the anomaly's observed and predicted properties.
